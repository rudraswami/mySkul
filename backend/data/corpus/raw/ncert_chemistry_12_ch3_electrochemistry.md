# Electrochemistry

## Electrochemical Cells

**Galvanic Cell (Voltaic):**
- Converts chemical energy to electrical energy
- Spontaneous reaction (ΔG < 0)
- Anode: Oxidation (negative terminal)
- Cathode: Reduction (positive terminal)

**Electrolytic Cell:**
- Converts electrical energy to chemical energy
- Non-spontaneous reaction (ΔG > 0)
- Anode: Oxidation (positive terminal)
- Cathode: Reduction (negative terminal)

**Cell Notation:**
Anode | Anode solution || Cathode solution | Cathode

Example: Zn | Zn²⁺(aq) || Cu²⁺(aq) | Cu

## Electrode Potential

**Standard Electrode Potential (E°):**
Potential of electrode when all species at unit activity (1 M, 1 atm), 25°C

**Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE):**
Reference electrode, E° = 0.00 V
Pt | H₂(1 atm) | H⁺(1 M)

**Cell EMF:**
E°_cell = E°_cathode - E°_anode

**Predicting Reactions:**
- Higher E°: Better oxidizing agent (gets reduced)
- Lower E°: Better reducing agent (gets oxidized)

## Nernst Equation

Relates cell potential to concentration.

**For a cell:**
E_cell = E°_cell - (RT/nF) ln Q

At 25°C:
E_cell = E°_cell - (0.059/n) log Q

**For electrode:**
E = E° - (0.059/n) log [reduced]/[oxidized]

**At Equilibrium:**
E_cell = 0, E°_cell = (0.059/n) log K

## Relationship Between E°, ΔG°, and K

**ΔG° = -nFE°**

where:
- n = number of electrons transferred
- F = Faraday constant = 96500 C/mol

**ΔG° = -RT ln K**

**E°_cell = (RT/nF) ln K = (0.059/n) log K** (at 25°C)

## Conductance

**Conductance (G):**
G = 1/R (Siemens, S)

**Conductivity (κ):**
κ = 1/ρ = G × (l/A) = G × cell constant

Units: S/m or S/cm

**Molar Conductivity (Λₘ):**
Λₘ = κ/C

where C = concentration in mol/m³

Units: S·m²/mol

## Variation of Conductivity

**Strong Electrolytes:**
Λₘ increases slightly with dilution
Λₘ = Λ°ₘ - A√C (Debye-Hückel-Onsager)

**Weak Electrolytes:**
Λₘ increases significantly with dilution

**At infinite dilution:**
Λ°ₘ represents complete dissociation

## Kohlrausch's Law

At infinite dilution, molar conductivity is sum of individual ionic conductivities.

**Formula:**
Λ°ₘ = ν₊λ°₊ + ν₋λ°₋

**Applications:**
1. Finding Λ°ₘ of weak electrolytes
2. Calculating degree of dissociation: α = Λₘ/Λ°ₘ
3. Finding dissociation constant: K = Cα²/(1-α)

## Electrolysis

**Faraday's Laws:**

**First Law:**
Mass deposited ∝ Quantity of electricity
m = ZIt = ZQ

where Z = electrochemical equivalent

**Second Law:**
For same quantity of electricity, masses of different substances are in ratio of equivalent weights.
m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂

**Relations:**
Z = E/F = M/(nF)

1 Faraday = 96500 C = charge to deposit 1 equivalent

## Batteries

**Primary Cells:** Non-rechargeable
- Dry cell (Leclanché)
- Mercury cell

**Secondary Cells:** Rechargeable
- Lead-acid battery
- Nickel-cadmium
- Lithium-ion

**Lead-Acid Battery:**
- Anode: Pb
- Cathode: PbO₂
- Electrolyte: H₂SO₄
- E = 2V per cell

**Fuel Cells:**
- Continuous supply of reactants
- H₂-O₂ fuel cell
- High efficiency (40-70%)
