# Classification of Elements and Periodicity

## Modern Periodic Table

**Basis:** Elements arranged by increasing atomic number (Moseley's law)

**Structure:**
- 7 Periods (horizontal rows)
- 18 Groups (vertical columns)
- 4 Blocks: s, p, d, f

**Blocks:**
- s-block: Groups 1-2 (alkali, alkaline earth metals)
- p-block: Groups 13-18
- d-block: Groups 3-12 (transition metals)
- f-block: Lanthanides and Actinides

## Periodic Trends

**Atomic Radius:**
- Decreases across period (left to right)
- Increases down a group
- Reason: More protons = more attraction; More shells = larger size

**Ionic Radius:**
- Cation < Parent atom (loses electrons)
- Anion > Parent atom (gains electrons)
- Isoelectronic species: More protons = smaller radius

**Ionization Energy (IE):**
Energy to remove outermost electron from gaseous atom.

M(g) → M⁺(g) + e⁻

- Increases across period (more nuclear charge)
- Decreases down group (more shielding)
- IE₁ < IE₂ < IE₃ (successive IEs increase)

**Exceptions:**
- Be > B (2s² more stable than 2s² 2p¹)
- N > O (half-filled 2p³ more stable)

**Electron Affinity (EA):**
Energy released when electron added to gaseous atom.

X(g) + e⁻ → X⁻(g)

- Generally increases across period
- Cl has highest EA (-349 kJ/mol)
- Noble gases have positive EA (stable configuration)

**Electronegativity:**
Tendency to attract shared electrons in a bond.

- Increases across period
- Decreases down group
- Pauling scale: F = 4.0 (highest)

**Metallic Character:**
- Decreases across period
- Increases down group
- Metals: good conductors, malleable, positive electrodes

## Diagonal Relationship

Elements diagonally placed show similar properties.

**Examples:**
- Li ~ Mg
- Be ~ Al
- B ~ Si

**Reasons:**
- Similar charge/radius ratio
- Similar electronegativity

## Important Groups

**Group 1 (Alkali Metals):**
Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr
- Most reactive metals
- +1 oxidation state
- React violently with water

**Group 2 (Alkaline Earth Metals):**
Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra
- +2 oxidation state
- Less reactive than Group 1

**Group 17 (Halogens):**
F, Cl, Br, I, At
- Most reactive nonmetals
- -1 oxidation state
- Diatomic molecules

**Group 18 (Noble Gases):**
He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn
- Stable full outer shell
- Minimal reactivity
- Monatomic gases

## Anomalous Properties of First Element

First element of each group differs from others:
- Small size
- High electronegativity
- No d-orbitals available
- Higher ionization energy

**Examples:**
- Li forms covalent compounds (unlike Na, K)
- N₂ is very stable (unlike P₄)
- O exists as O₂ (unlike S₈)
