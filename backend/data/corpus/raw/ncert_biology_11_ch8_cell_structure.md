# Cell: The Unit of Life

## Cell Theory

**Key Points:**
1. All living organisms are composed of cells
2. Cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells (Virchow)

**Exceptions:**
- Viruses are acellular
- Some organisms are multinucleate (coenocytic)

## Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

**Prokaryotic Cells:**
- No true nucleus (nucleoid region)
- No membrane-bound organelles
- 70S ribosomes
- Cell wall present (peptidoglycan in bacteria)
- Examples: Bacteria, Cyanobacteria, Mycoplasma

**Eukaryotic Cells:**
- True nucleus with nuclear membrane
- Membrane-bound organelles present
- 80S ribosomes (70S in mitochondria, chloroplasts)
- Examples: Plants, Animals, Fungi, Protists

## Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane)

**Fluid Mosaic Model (Singer & Nicolson):**
- Phospholipid bilayer
- Proteins embedded (integral) or attached (peripheral)
- Cholesterol for stability
- Carbohydrates attached (glycoproteins, glycolipids)

**Functions:**
- Selective permeability
- Cell recognition
- Signal transduction
- Transport of substances

**Transport across membrane:**
- Passive: Diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion
- Active: Against concentration gradient, requires ATP

## Cell Wall

**Plant Cell Wall:**
- Primary wall: Cellulose, hemicellulose, pectin
- Secondary wall: Lignin (in mature cells)
- Middle lamella: Pectin (cement between cells)

**Functions:**
- Protection
- Shape maintenance
- Prevention of bursting in hypotonic solution

## Nucleus

**Components:**
- Nuclear envelope: Double membrane with pores
- Nucleoplasm: Matrix material
- Chromatin: DNA + histone proteins
- Nucleolus: rRNA synthesis, ribosome assembly

**Functions:**
- Genetic information storage
- Control of cell activities
- DNA replication
- Transcription

## Endoplasmic Reticulum

**Rough ER (RER):**
- Ribosomes attached
- Protein synthesis and processing
- Secretory proteins

**Smooth ER (SER):**
- No ribosomes
- Lipid synthesis
- Detoxification
- Calcium storage

## Golgi Apparatus

**Structure:**
- Stack of flattened cisternae
- Cis face (forming face): Receives from ER
- Trans face (maturing face): Sends to destination

**Functions:**
- Protein modification and packaging
- Glycosylation
- Lysosome formation
- Cell wall formation (in plants)

## Lysosomes

**Characteristics:**
- Single membrane
- Contain hydrolytic enzymes (pH ~5)
- "Suicidal bags" of cell

**Functions:**
- Intracellular digestion
- Autophagy (self-eating)
- Autolysis (cell death)
- Defense against pathogens

## Mitochondria

**Structure:**
- Double membrane
- Outer membrane: Smooth, porous
- Inner membrane: Folded into cristae
- Matrix: Enzymes, 70S ribosomes, circular DNA

**Functions:**
- Cellular respiration (ATP synthesis)
- Krebs cycle in matrix
- ETC on inner membrane
- "Powerhouse of cell"

**Semi-autonomous:**
- Own DNA and ribosomes
- Self-replicating
- Maternal inheritance

## Chloroplasts

**Structure:**
- Double membrane
- Thylakoids: Stacked into grana
- Stroma: Enzyme-rich matrix
- 70S ribosomes, circular DNA

**Functions:**
- Photosynthesis
- Light reactions in thylakoids
- Dark reactions (Calvin cycle) in stroma

## Ribosomes

**Structure:**
- 70S in prokaryotes (50S + 30S)
- 80S in eukaryotes (60S + 40S)
- Made of rRNA and proteins

**Functions:**
- Protein synthesis
- Translation of mRNA

## Cytoskeleton

**Components:**
- Microtubules: Tubulin, largest (25nm)
- Microfilaments: Actin, smallest (7nm)
- Intermediate filaments: Various proteins (10nm)

**Functions:**
- Cell shape
- Movement
- Cell division
- Intracellular transport
