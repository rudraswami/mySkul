# Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

## Classification

**Haloalkanes:** Halogen attached to sp³ carbon
- Alkyl halides: R-X (X = F, Cl, Br, I)
- Primary (1°): Halogen on primary carbon
- Secondary (2°): Halogen on secondary carbon
- Tertiary (3°): Halogen on tertiary carbon

**Haloarenes:** Halogen attached to benzene ring
- Aryl halides: Ar-X

## Nomenclature

**IUPAC:**
- Name halogen as prefix (fluoro, chloro, bromo, iodo)
- Number to give lowest position
- Examples:
  - CH₃CH₂Cl: Chloroethane
  - CH₃CHClCH₃: 2-Chloropropane
  - C₆H₅Br: Bromobenzene

**Common Names:**
- CH₃Cl: Methyl chloride
- CHCl₃: Chloroform
- CCl₄: Carbon tetrachloride
- CH₂Cl₂: Methylene chloride

## Physical Properties

**Boiling Points:**
- Higher than parent hydrocarbons
- Increase with molecular mass
- Order: R-I > R-Br > R-Cl > R-F

**Density:**
- CH₃Cl, CH₃Br: Less dense than water
- Higher alkyl halides: More dense than water

**Solubility:**
- Insoluble in water (non-polar)
- Soluble in organic solvents

## Preparation Methods

**1. From Alcohols:**
- R-OH + HX → R-X + H₂O (order: HI > HBr > HCl)
- R-OH + PCl₅ → R-Cl + POCl₃ + HCl
- R-OH + SOCl₂ → R-Cl + SO₂ + HCl (best method)
- 3R-OH + PBr₃ → 3R-Br + H₃PO₃

**2. From Hydrocarbons:**
- Free radical halogenation (UV light)
- CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl (substitution)
- Alkene + HX → Alkyl halide (addition)

**3. Halogen Exchange (Finkelstein):**
R-X + NaI → R-I + NaX (in acetone)

**4. Swarts Reaction:**
R-Cl + AgF → R-F + AgCl (for fluorides)

## Chemical Reactions

**1. Nucleophilic Substitution:**

**SN2 Mechanism:**
- Bimolecular, one-step
- Backside attack by nucleophile
- Inversion of configuration (Walden inversion)
- Rate = k[R-X][Nu⁻]
- Order of reactivity: 1° > 2° > 3°

**SN1 Mechanism:**
- Unimolecular, two-step
- Carbocation intermediate
- Racemization
- Rate = k[R-X]
- Order of reactivity: 3° > 2° > 1°

**2. Elimination Reactions (E1, E2):**
- Form alkenes
- Saytzeff's rule: Major product is more substituted alkene
- Strong base, high temperature favors elimination

**3. Grignard Reagent Formation:**
R-X + Mg → R-MgX (in dry ether)

**Uses of Grignard:**
- With H₂O → R-H
- With CO₂ → R-COOH
- With aldehydes → secondary alcohols
- With ketones → tertiary alcohols

## Haloarenes

**Reactivity:**
- Less reactive than alkyl halides toward nucleophilic substitution
- Reason: Resonance stabilization, sp² carbon

**Electrophilic Substitution:**
- Halogen is deactivating but ortho-para directing
- Due to resonance donation > inductive withdrawal

**Reactions:**
- Nitration, halogenation, sulfonation (o,p products)
- Friedel-Crafts (with catalyst)

**Nucleophilic Substitution:**
- Requires very strong conditions
- Benzyne mechanism
- Activating groups (NO₂) at ortho/para help

## Polyhalogen Compounds

**Dichloromethane (CH₂Cl₂):**
- Solvent, paint remover
- Low toxicity

**Chloroform (CHCl₃):**
- Anesthetic (historical)
- Solvent for fats
- Oxidizes to phosgene in air

**Carbon Tetrachloride (CCl₄):**
- Fire extinguisher (historical)
- Dry cleaning
- Toxic to liver

**Freons (CFCs):**
- Refrigerants
- Ozone layer depletion

**DDT:**
- Insecticide
- Non-biodegradable
- Bioaccumulation problem
