# Cell Cycle and Cell Division

## Cell Cycle

**Definition:**
Sequence of events from formation of cell to its division into daughter cells.

**Phases:**
1. **Interphase** (95% of cycle)
   - G1 phase: Cell growth, protein synthesis
   - S phase: DNA replication
   - G2 phase: Preparation for division

2. **M Phase** (Mitotic phase)
   - Division of nucleus (karyokinesis)
   - Division of cytoplasm (cytokinesis)

**Quiescent Stage (G0):**
- Cells exit cycle temporarily
- Can re-enter if stimulated
- Example: Neurons stay in G0 permanently

## Mitosis

**Purpose:**
- Growth
- Repair
- Asexual reproduction
- Maintains chromosome number

**Stages:**

**1. Prophase:**
- Chromatin condenses to chromosomes
- Nuclear membrane starts breaking
- Centrioles move to poles
- Spindle fibers begin forming
- Nucleolus disappears

**2. Metaphase:**
- Chromosomes align at equator (metaphase plate)
- Spindle fibers attach to kinetochores
- Maximum chromosome condensation
- Best stage for karyotyping

**3. Anaphase:**
- Centromeres split
- Sister chromatids separate
- Move to opposite poles
- Shortest phase
- Spindle fibers shorten

**4. Telophase:**
- Chromosomes reach poles
- Nuclear membrane reforms
- Chromosomes decondense
- Nucleolus reappears
- Spindle fibers disappear

**Cytokinesis:**
- Animal cells: Cleavage furrow (centripetal)
- Plant cells: Cell plate formation (centrifugal)

## Meiosis

**Purpose:**
- Gamete formation
- Reduces chromosome number by half
- Creates genetic variation

**Meiosis I (Reductional Division):**

**Prophase I:**
Longest and most complex phase.

- **Leptotene:** Chromosomes become visible
- **Zygotene:** Homologous pairing (synapsis), bivalent formation
- **Pachytene:** Crossing over occurs, recombination
- **Diplotene:** Chiasmata visible, partial separation
- **Diakinesis:** Bivalents move to periphery, nuclear membrane breaks

**Metaphase I:**
- Bivalents align at equator
- Random orientation (independent assortment)

**Anaphase I:**
- Homologous chromosomes separate
- Sister chromatids stay together
- Reduction of chromosome number

**Telophase I:**
- Chromosomes reach poles
- Two haploid cells formed

**Meiosis II (Similar to Mitosis):**
- No DNA replication between divisions
- Sister chromatids separate
- Results in 4 haploid cells

## Significance of Meiosis

1. **Maintains chromosome number** across generations
2. **Genetic variation** through:
   - Crossing over (new allele combinations)
   - Independent assortment
   - Random fertilization

3. **Formation of gametes:**
   - Spermatogenesis: 1 cell → 4 sperms
   - Oogenesis: 1 cell → 1 ovum + 3 polar bodies

## Key Differences

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Purpose | Growth, repair | Gamete formation |
| Divisions | 1 | 2 |
| Daughter cells | 2 (diploid) | 4 (haploid) |
| Genetic makeup | Identical | Different |
| Crossing over | No | Yes (Prophase I) |
| Synapsis | No | Yes |
| Duration | Shorter | Longer |

## Cell Cycle Regulation

**Checkpoints:**
- G1/S checkpoint: DNA damage check
- G2/M checkpoint: DNA replication complete
- Metaphase checkpoint: Proper spindle attachment

**Regulators:**
- Cyclins: Concentration varies with phase
- CDKs (Cyclin-dependent kinases): Always present
- Cyclin-CDK complexes drive cell cycle

**Cancer:**
- Uncontrolled cell division
- Loss of checkpoint control
- Mutations in tumor suppressors (p53) or oncogenes
